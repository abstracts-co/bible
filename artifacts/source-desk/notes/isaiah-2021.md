# Who wrote the Great Isaiah Scroll?

Popović, Dhali & Schomaker (2021), *PLOS ONE* 16(4): e0249769. [doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0249769](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0249769)

**The question.** 1QIsaa looks like it was written in one near-uniform hand. Is that one scribe varying a bit, or two scribes writing very alike? And if two, does the change line up with the two halves of the scroll? (p. 2)

**What they did.** Measured the letter shapes in images of all 54 columns with several different feature methods, first without assuming anyone's identity, then with statistical tests. The images were Brill's; columns 16 and 46 weren't in that set (p. 6).

**What they report.** Columns from the two halves land in separate zones of the plots, and a switch point in the column series shows up around columns 27–29 (pp. 1, 22). The figure here is the plainest version: each column's nearest neighbour in handwriting tends to sit on its own side. Their preferred reading is two main scribes working closely in the same style (pp. 21–22).

**What it doesn't say.** Nothing about *when*. This is writer identification, not dating. They're also clear that the methods "do not give certainty of identification but statistically proven probabilities" (p. 24), and they list three things that could affect the results: the scroll's degradation, the pens and ink, and imperfect letter extraction (p. 23).

**Still open for me:** the break they found is where the scroll already has a sewn seam and a gap at the bottom of column 27 (p. 2). How much does the handwriting evidence add to what the physical seam already suggested? I'd like to read a palaeographer's response.
