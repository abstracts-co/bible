# KhQ 1474, a jar with lid

The jar our [3D model](https://abstracts.co/a/bible/artifacts/jar) is built from.

**The record.** Library of Congress exhibition, *Scrolls from the Dead Sea* (1993), "Artifacts from the Qumran site": "Jar with Lid. Pottery. First century B.C.E.–first century C.E. KhQ 1474", courtesy of the Israel Antiquities Authority, exhibit nos. 57, 58. [loc.gov/exhibits/scrolls/art1.html](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/scrolls/art1.html)

**Measurements** (read from our jar's `dimensions.json`, which copies the catalogue): {dimensions: see artifacts/jar/dimensions.json}

**What the caption claims.** Some of the scrolls found in 1947 were in cylindrical jars "of this type", and the caption calls them "unknown elsewhere". It says "many authorities" take the same jars turning up in the settlement and in the caves as convincing evidence linking the two, and that they were "probably manufactured locally".

**Where that's moved on.** Dennis Mizzi's 2026 chapter (cited in our [cutaway's sources](https://github.com/abstracts-co/bible/blob/main/artifacts/jar-anatomy/SOURCES.md)) reports a few cylindrical jars from Jericho, Masada, Qalandiya and Tel Esur. So "unknown elsewhere" is a 1993 exhibition caption, not where things stand now. Whether the jars still tie the caves to the settlement is an archaeology question I'm not qualified to settle.

**What our model is.** Height and diameter from this record. The profile, wall thickness, colour and lid fit are guesses, listed in the [jar's sources](https://github.com/abstracts-co/bible/blob/main/artifacts/jar/SOURCES.md). It isn't a scan.
