# 1QIsaa, the Great Isaiah Scroll

Cave 1, Qumran. One of the first seven scrolls found ([IAA, Discovery Sites](https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/learn-about-the-scrolls/discovery-sites)).

**The object.** 54 columns of Hebrew on a scroll 7.34 m long and about 26 cm high on average. Between columns 27 and 28 two sheets are sewn together, and the bottom of column 27 has a three-line gap. The spelling and word forms differ in the second half, and there are blank spaces (Popović, Dhali & Schomaker 2021, p. 2).

**Two papers on it.** The 2021 handwriting study argues for two main scribes, splitting around columns 27–29 (pp. 1, 22). The 2025 Enoch study dates both halves to the same period, 180–100 BCE, and says neither half looks significantly later than the other (Popović et al. 2025, p. 7).

**A number that doesn't match.** The 2021 paper gives an average height of 26 cm. The Israel Museum's Digital Dead Sea Scrolls page, which our jar cutaway cites, gives 22–25 cm. I haven't worked out why. It could be measured at different points, or include different edges. If you know, please say.

**Images.** I haven't put a photo of the scroll here because I haven't found one we're clearly allowed to republish. The Israel Museum's [digital scroll](http://dss.collections.imj.org.il/isaiah) lets you read it column by column.

**Still open:** if two scribes split the work at a sheet join, does that tell us anything about how a long scroll was produced? That needs someone who knows the scribal-practice literature.
