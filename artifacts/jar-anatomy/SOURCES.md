## Where sources differ

- **Jar and lid sizes.** KhQ 1474 (1) and the Met jar (2) give jar H 49.8–62 cm and Ø 24–27.9 cm, lid H 5–7.7 cm and Ø 17.8–18.2 cm. These are two catalogued objects, not the full range of every Qumran jar.
- **Found only at Qumran?** The 1993 exhibition text (1) calls the jars "unknown elsewhere"; Mizzi (3) reports a few examples from Jericho, Masada, Qalandiya and Tel Esur. The figure makes no claim either way.
- **Scroll wrappers.** Crowfoot treated decorated and plain cloths alike as wrappers; Taylor et al. (4) note that this is not certain and that the fringed cloths may have been cut down from clothing.

## What is reconstructed

- **Drawn sizes:** jar 56 cm high and 26 cm across; lid 6.5 × 18 cm; scroll roll 24 cm high. All fall inside the ranges above.
- **Not published for these objects, so assumed:** a wall thickness of about 0.9 cm, the neck and the flat seat the lid rests on, the jar's exact curve, the roll's diameter (6.8 cm) and a single scroll standing in the jar. How many scrolls a jar held is not known from these sources.
- **Handles:** two loop handles show in the Met photograph (2); the back pair is assumed. Not every cylindrical jar has handles.
- **Linen:** the wrapper is drawn closing over the end of the roll with its corners folded to the centre, after the description in (4). Its thickness is exaggerated so it can be seen. A thong and slotted tab (1) are drawn under the linen, which the sources describe for scrolls in general, not for this wrapper.
- **Display stand, plinth and label plate** are modern museum furniture, drawn to hold the round-based jar. They are not archaeological.
- **Scale bar:** the 10 cm bar lies on the plinth in the drawing's own units. It reads true for horizontal lengths; the isometric view shortens heights to 87% (cos 30°).
