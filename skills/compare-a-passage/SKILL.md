---
name: compare-a-passage
description: Put published translations of a passage side by side for a/bible. Keep each quotation exact, record the edition and license, and mark where your own reading begins.
---

Pick a short passage. Three verses is plenty; nobody reads a side-by-side of a whole chapter.

**Quoting.** Copy each translation exactly, verse boundaries included, from a named edition you can link to. Keep its punctuation and its own footnotes if you show them. Write down the edition or release, the download URL, the date you got it and, if you downloaded a file, its SHA-256. If you change a single word of a translation, it isn't that translation any more; the World English Bible in particular asks that changed text not be called the WEB.

**Rights.** Public domain and CC0 translations can be quoted in full. Most modern translations can't, beyond short fair quotation. Check the publisher's terms page before you put one in. If it isn't clear, link to it instead.

**Differences.** Point to where the translations differ and ask why. A difference can be English style, a different base text, or a translator's call, and from the English alone you often can't tell which. Say which you think it is only if you've looked at the textual notes or an edition of the original, and cite them.

**Your own reading.** Keep it visibly separate from the quoted translations, with a literal gloss apart from any smoother version. A new claim about the Greek, Hebrew or Aramaic goes through the translation route in CONTRIBUTING.md and waits for a language reviewer.

Don't rank translations, and don't ask people to vote on one.
