---
name: make-a-reading-guide
description: Make a short reading route for a/bible. Start from a real question and give a few useful stops, not a bibliography nobody will read.
---

A guide answers one question someone actually has, like "what's the deal with the scroll jars?" or "how do they date these things?". If you can't say the question in a sentence, it isn't ready.

Three to five stops. For each:

- what to open: an artifact here, a paper with pages, a museum page, a chapter
- why it's next
- one thing to look for, or one question to carry to the next stop

Put the reader's time first. Say roughly how long a stop takes if it's long. Give page numbers for books and papers. Prefer things people can open for free, and say so when something is paywalled.

Link out to videos and images on their original pages unless their license lets us copy them. Reuse the community's own posters if you like, with their credits carried along.

End with what's still open: the questions the stops don't settle, and one or two serious positions you didn't have room for.

Don't write it as if a panel of experts endorsed it. It's a route you (or we) found useful. If you adapted someone's Wall answer, ask them first and link it.

Guides are usually a Markdown `document` artifact. Use the guide-answer PR template.
